Why measure it

Founders measure revenue. Athletes measure splits. Investors measure returns. Most high performers never measure the thing everything else depends on: how sharp they are on a given day.

How it works
  1. Take the test. Tap the green TAP circle, and hold when you see the amber HOLD ring. About 60 seconds.
  2. Answer 5 quick questions. Sleep, movement, late screens, workload and alcohol. One tap each.
  3. Get your NEURL Score. 70% from the test, compared with your own best, and 30% from your habits over the last 7 days.
  4. Unlock your stats. Your stat card rates your reaction, focus, consistency, control and stamina against your own normal range, like a check-up for how you’re running today.
  5. See what moves it. Insights shows how sleep, activity, screens and workload line up with your stats, and gives you one change to try.

How your score works
  • One score, two parts. 70% comes from today’s test and 30% from your habits over the last 7 days. If you haven’t logged habits, your score is the test alone.
  • Five measures: speed, your slowest taps, consistency (how even your taps are), lapses (taps slower than half a second), and control (taps when you should hold).
  • Scored like the research version. Speed counts responses per second, your slowest 10% of taps are scored on their own because tiredness shows there first, and taps under 0.1 seconds count as guesses.
  • The test is measured against you. Each day is compared with your own best result from the last 28 days, so 100% means you matched your best.
  • Habits use simple guidelines. For example, 7+ hours of sleep and about 150 active minutes a week. You need 3 logged days for habits to count.
  • 7-test baseline. During your first 7 tests you still get a score, compared with your best so far. After that, your peak locks in.
  • Saved on this device. Phones add different delays, so using another phone or browser starts a new baseline.
  • Feel vs. measured. Rating how alert you feel before the test never changes your score. It shows whether your sense of alertness matches how you actually perform.
  • Four states. Peak is 81–100, Strong is 66–80, Recovering is 46–65 and Rebuild is 0–45. Your state shows as a tag under your score.
  • Inspired by reaction tests used in sleep and fatigue research. A performance tool, not a medical one.
